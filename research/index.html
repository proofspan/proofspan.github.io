Research

What it means to verify a number.

Notes on the principles behind the engines. These are design commitments, not aspirations: each one is enforced in code and covered by tests.

  1. 01

    Three-valued verdicts

    A verifier that answers only yes or no is forced to guess when evidence is missing. Proofspan's checks return PASS, FAIL or INCONCLUSIVE, and INCONCLUSIVE is a first-class result: zero and negative bases, mismatched currencies, overlapping or dissimilar reporting periods, and absent values all end there rather than in a fabricated number.

  2. 02

    Tolerance from stated precision

    Floats are never compared with equality. The default tolerance is half a unit in the last decimal place the claim was stated with, so “20%” is consistent with a true 20.4% while “20.0%” is not. Explicit absolute or relative tolerances override this, and the tolerance used is recorded in the result.

  3. 03

    Availability, not period end

    A fiscal year ending in December does not make its annual report knowable in January. Point-in-time checks use publication and availability timestamps only; the reporting period is never a proxy. When only a calendar date is known and that date is the cutoff day, the verdict is INCONCLUSIVE.

  4. 04

    Restatements as leakage

    Figures are matched against a document's full revision history. The earliest revision carrying the figure governs: if it was published after the cutoff, using it is look-ahead leakage, and the report names the value that was actually knowable.

  5. 05

    Reports that carry their assumptions

    Every statistical result states the metric, the sample size actually used after dropping missing values, a mathematical interpretation, the assumptions it rests on, and warnings. Resampling takes an explicit seed. Chronological validation runs a leakage guard before any model is fitted.

  6. 06

    Cross-checked numerics

    Known-answer tests pin every calculation to values derivable by hand, and the statistical layer is cross-checked against SciPy: binomial coverage tests, chi-square calibration tails, Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistics and bootstrap intervals agree with the reference implementations.
